The Pluto gets a wider tuning range and a live view. The RFOne and Airspy firmware and the host libraries
are unchanged from v0.2.0 (the images rebuild byte for byte).

> **Unofficial.** Corvus is not affiliated with, endorsed by or supported by HydraSDR, Airspy,
> Great Scott Gadgets or Analog Devices. **Flashing unofficial firmware is at your own risk.** Back up
> your board's flash first, and read [firmware/README.md](https://github.com/auggiesoft/corvus/blob/v0.3.1/firmware/README.md#flashing).

## What's new

- **Wider tuning on the Pluto: about 18 MHz to 6.18 GHz** (it was 70 MHz to 6 GHz). `corvus-pluto` 0.2.1
  pushes the AD936x's synthesizer past the driver's 46.88 MHz–6 GHz where the chip locks (it checks at
  start-up; 46–6150 MHz on the Pluto+ tested), and takes steps further out at the nearest end, shifted
  digitally, which reaches half the RF bandwidth further. None of this is in the chip's data sheet: expect
  less accuracy towards the ends. Full range at ~3,100 steps/s, 20 passes a second over Ethernet.
- **Live view (Pluto):** the Range card's Live switch, or **L** over the spectrum (centred on the cursor).
  The board stays on one frequency and streams; pick a span of 50, 25, 12, 6, 3 or 1 MHz and the lowest
  sample rate that covers it is used, for bins down to 0.94 kHz. About 3,800 updates a second at the widest
  spans; at 15.36 MSPS and below about 94 % of the signal is captured, nearly gapless. Shift+arrows move it
  a span; L goes back to the sweep it came from. Live views for the other boards are to follow.
- **New Pluto rates:** 15.36, 7.68 and 3.84 MSPS (for sweeps too: narrower steps, finer bins).
- **Fewer false alarms:** blocks of the previous plan still in flight when the plan changes are dropped
  instead of counted as gaps.
- **Release builds** run on a self-hosted runner, with fallbacks for the toolchain and Ubuntu archive
  downloads.

Update the board with the new `corvus-pluto` (`install.sh <board address>`): Corvus Sweep 0.3.1 also works
with 0.2.0 on the board (tested), over the driver's 46.88 MHz–6 GHz. Corvus Sweep needs a Vulkan or GL driver (DirectX 12 on
Windows). Source for every binary is this repository at tag `v0.3.1`; each archive's NOTICE.txt lists the licences.
